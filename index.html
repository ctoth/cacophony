Cacophony Playground

A hands-on, screen-reader-operable demonstration of what cacophony can do. Every demo is meant to be heard: press a control and listen. Non-audible measurements (e.g. the loudness meter) are computed and announced as text.

Audio is off. Activate “Enable audio” to begin.

5. Filter chain (biquad)

6. FDN reverb (Schlecht 2019 + Fagerström 2020)

7. ADAA distortion (Parker 2016)

A/B clean vs. antialiased waveshaping. Raise drive for more grit.

8. Dynamics (Giannoulis 2012)

A compressor on the loop bus. A/B to hear the level tamed.

11. Tremolo / auto-pan (Dattorro 1997, p.776 quadrature LFO)

LFO-driven amplitude modulation. Choose the LFO shape and the stereo mode, then A/B against the dry loop. Mono pulses both channels together; quadrature and auto-pan offset the channels (most audible on a stereo source). Controls apply on the next “Play with tremolo”.

5.0 Hz
50%

12. Pitch-shift (Laroche-Dolson 1999)

Shift the loop’s pitch without changing its speed. Octave up, down, or unison — live.

13. Time-stretch (Průša 2022, PGHI)

Change the loop’s duration while keeping its pitch. Compare original to half- and double-length.

14. Ambisonic → binaural (Ahrens 2022)

A mono tone is encoded to first-order ambisonics at a chosen azimuth, then decoded to binaural through the HRIR. Move the azimuth and hear it travel around you. (This exercises the real decoder audio graph.)

+90° (left)
